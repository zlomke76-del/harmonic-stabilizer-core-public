9. EFFECT ON THE BILATERAL BOUNDARY QUESTION
======================================================================

The following proposition is now weakened further:

"Harmonic lacks the mechanism needed to make changed authority operative."

T4 v1.1 demonstrates that frozen Harmonic already contains an operative
mechanism once changed authority is bound into its runtime-consumed state.

The surviving question therefore shifts upstream from mechanism existence to
representation responsibility:

WHAT ENSURES THAT A LEGITIMATELY ESTABLISHED INSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY CHANGE IS
CORRECTLY AND TIMELY BOUND INTO THE RUNTIME STATE CONSUMED BY AN ALREADY-
EXISTING AUTHORITY-COHERENCE MECHANISM?

This is a responsibility / representation / integration question.

T4 v1.1 does not establish that this function is unowned.

It may be owned by:
- an upstream authority source;
- a policy administration/distribution function;
- an integration or adapter layer;
- an access-control or capability-revocation mechanism;
- a configuration/state-distribution architecture;
- another established governance/control architecture.

Accordingly:

NO GAP OR SEAM CONSTRUCT IS ADMITTED.

The next burden is established-owner / absorption testing.

T4 v1.1 succeeds as a discriminator.

1. Representation activation materially changes the Harmonic authority and
